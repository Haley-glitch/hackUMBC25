High-Yield Savings Account

A savings account that offers significantly higher interest rates than traditional savings accounts, typically 10-25x higher than national averages.

Expected Return:
4.0% - 5.0% APY
Min Investment:
$0 - $100
Risk: 5/10 High Liquidity

Index Funds

Mutual funds or ETFs that track a specific market index like the S&P 500, providing instant diversification across hundreds of stocks.

Expected Return:
8% - 12% annually
Min Investment:
$1 - $3,000
Risk: 8/10 High Liquidity

Individual Stocks

Shares of ownership in individual companies. Requires research and offers potential for high returns but with significant risk.

Expected Return:
-100% to +1000%+
Min Investment:
$1 (fractional shares)
Risk: 4/10 High Liquidity

Target-Date Funds

Mutual funds that automatically adjust their asset allocation to become more conservative as they approach a target date, typically retirement.

Expected Return:
6% - 10% annually
Min Investment:
$0 - $1,000
Risk: 3/10 Medium Liquidity

Corporate Bonds

Debt securities issued by corporations to raise capital. Bondholders receive regular interest payments and the principal back at maturity.

Expected Return:
4% - 7% annually
Min Investment:
$1,000 - $5,000
Risk: 5/10 High Liquidity

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

Companies that own, operate, or finance income-producing real estate across various property sectors.

Expected Return:
7% - 12% annually
Min Investment:
$1 - $100

College Student Investment Guide

Smart financial moves to set yourself up for success, even on a tight budget

Start with a High-Yield Savings Account

Build your emergency fund first. Aim for $1,000-$2,500 as your initial goal.

  • Use automatic transfers
  • Start with $25-50/month
  • Keep it separate from spending money

Learn Investment Basics

Understanding compound interest and dollar-cost averaging will serve you for life.

  • Read 'The Simple Path to Wealth'
  • Follow reputable financial blogs
  • Take a personal finance course

Consider Low-Cost Index Funds

Once you have emergency savings, index funds are perfect for beginners.

  • Start with broad market ETFs
  • Look for expense ratios under 0.20%
  • Use dollar-cost averaging

Take Advantage of Time

You have the most valuable asset: time. Even small amounts grow significantly.

  • Invest consistently, not perfectly
  • $50/month can become $175,000 in 40 years
  • Start now, increase later
